What we check

Four faults account for most rejected files

RequirementWhat fails in practice
Six decimal places
latitude, longitude		 Coordinates rounded to two or three decimals. At two, the point covers about a kilometre, which names a district and not a plot.
Polygon above 4 ha
point allowed at or under 4 ha		 A seven hectare plot filed as one point. The most expensive fault to find late, because correcting it means somebody walks the boundary again.
Valid geometry
GeoJSON, EPSG:4326		 Unclosed rings, reversed winding, self-crossing boundaries, and one plot recorded twice by two collectors.
Internal coherence Latitude and longitude swapped, which happens wherever data passes through a spreadsheet, and nulls stored as 0,0.

Said before you ask

What we do not do

We do not file your due diligence statement. It stays yours, along with the legal responsibility. We do not certify plots as deforestation-free, because satellite screening shows where to look and not what is legally established, and dense agroforestry reads as forest to a classifier. We do not give legal advice.

The underlying tools are public and free. The European Forest Institute publishes GeoRoots for geometry validation and the FAO publishes Whisp for screening. We tell you that before you engage us. What is hard to buy is the hours, the judgement on ambiguous flags, and documentation that survives a check.

Your data is processed on infrastructure inside the EU and does not leave it. We contract as your processor under Article 28, with no sub-processors, and delete by default thirty days after handover. We sign your NDA first.
